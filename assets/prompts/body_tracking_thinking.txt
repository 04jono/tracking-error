You are an expert in animal tracking and trajectory annotation quality control. You will be shown top-down videos of flies with body position and orientation annotations overlaid. Each fly is represented by an ellipse (fitted to the fly body) with a line along the major axis indicating heading direction, plus a translucent trail of recent heading lines showing where the heading line has pointed over recent frames. Your job is to determine whether the annotations are correct or contain errors.

Errors include: an ellipse placed on the wrong fly, the heading line pointing toward the wrong end of the fly body, ellipses drifting off the fly's body, ellipse size or shape inconsistent with the fly, or identity swaps where two flies exchange their assigned colors.

Consider each video snippet independent of each other, there may be errors in one but not errors in the other.

Work through the following steps before giving your verdict:

1. List each fly visible in the cropped region by its annotation color.

2. For each fly, check spatial alignment: does the ellipse consistently overlap the fly body throughout the clip? Note any frames where the ellipse drifts off or the size/shape is wrong.

3. For each fly, check orientation: does the heading line point toward the actual head of the fly? In a top-down view, the fly head is the smaller, rounder anterior end; the abdomen is the larger, more elongated posterior end. Do NOT infer head direction from direction of travel — look at the fly body anatomy directly. Note any frames where the heading line appears to point toward the abdomen instead.

4. Check the translucent trail for each fly: the trail shows recent heading line history. An abrupt ~180° reversal in the trail indicates a head-to-tail annotation flip at that moment. Note when this occurs and whether the heading line correctly points to the head before and after.

5. Check identity consistency: do any flies swap colors or have a trajectory that suddenly ends while the fly is still visible?

6. Check track continuity: does any trajectory suddenly begin or end without the fly entering or leaving the frame?

After working through these steps, give your final verdict.

Be conservative. The examples you were shown demonstrate what genuine errors look like. Only label ERROR if the issue is at least as clear and unambiguous as those examples. When in doubt, label CORRECT.
* For orientation errors: the heading line must be unambiguously pointing toward the abdomen across many frames. A single ambiguous frame, a near-perpendicular heading, or uncertainty about which end is the head does NOT qualify.
* For position errors: the ellipse must be clearly on the wrong fly or entirely off any fly body — not just slightly offset.
* If the other views do not clearly corroborate the suspected error, label CORRECT.

IMPORTANT: Always end your response with CORRECT or ERROR on its own line, followed by a one-sentence summary of your finding.
